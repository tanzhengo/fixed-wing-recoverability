\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Common Planner and Execution Model}
The primary library contains 29 horizontal directions and seven altitude targets, giving 203 controls of duration $H=3$ s. The 15 continuation controls combine five horizontal direction fractions $\{0,-0.5,0.5,-1,1\}$ of a $45^\circ$ span with level, upper and lower altitude targets over $B=6$ s. The initial airspeed is 15.5 m/s at numerically solved level-flight trim. Roll is limited to $35^\circ$, climb and descent to $20^\circ$, and angle of attack to $[-20^\circ,25^\circ]$. Planner integration uses forward Euler with a 0.02 s step.

All methods apply identical primary constraints, continuation banks, collision inflation and task costs. The goal is $(250,0,20)$ m, with a 15 m horizontal tolerance and 3 m altitude tolerance. The control interval is 0.2 s, and the episode timeout is 40 s. The principal suite provides complete static obstacle geometry. All reported learning experiments use the full supplied geometry and disable the execution cache.

An independently implemented execution plant integrates the same nominal point-mass equations using fourth-order Runge--Kutta with a 0.005 s step. Four execution conditions are evaluated. The nominal condition uses unchanged parameters. Model mismatch multiplies actuator time constants by 1.25, lift coefficients by 0.97 and drag coefficients by 1.10. Crosswind adds a lateral ground velocity of 1 m/s. Command delay applies the previous control for 0.1 s and the new control for the remaining 0.1 s of each update. The planner always receives the actual state at the next update.

\subsection{Training, Validation and Test Maps}
Four procedural map families contain staggered pillars, a lateral obstacle wall, an offset cul-de-sac, and finite-height cylinders with floating spheres. The first three use altitude bounds of 16--24 m; the spatial family uses 5--45 m. Independent seed ranges define 160 training maps, 32 validation maps and 32 test maps, balanced across the four families. The test maps are unseen instances of these families. None of the 24 maps used to develop the earlier nonlearning baseline belongs to these splits.

Training and validation each sample 24 states per map. Position varies over $p_x\in[0,65]$ m and $p_y\in[-12,12]$ m; heading varies within $\pm0.3$ rad. Airspeed is sampled from 14.8--16.2 m/s, flight-path angle within $\pm0.04$ rad, and roll within $\pm0.18$ rad. Angle of attack is perturbed by up to 0.015 rad from trim, and thrust by up to 10\%. Altitude is 20 m. The preceding command holds the sampled actuator state. This generates 298,418 feasible-primary examples from 3,840 training states and 53,117 examples from 768 validation states. The respective numbers of positive continuation labels are 127,311 and 20,507. States with no feasible generated primary remain in the dataset audit, although they supply no candidate labels.

The network is trained for 60 epochs using Adam, a learning rate of $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$, and batches of 2,048 examples. The random seed is fixed at 20261030. Checkpoints are selected by the minimum validation loss in~\eqref{eq:learning_loss}. The geometry model selects epoch 60. The geometry ablation uses the same architecture, optimizer, split and seed, masks the 75 obstacle-clearance channels, and selects epoch 55. Its exported first-layer weights on these channels are zeroed, so the ablation is identical in training and C++ execution. The remaining inputs still include the target, altitude bounds and primary cost.

We evaluate $\beta\in\{0,0.5,1\}$ on validation queries. A shared value maximizes mean discovery recall over Learned and Hybrid schedules under both $K=12$ and $W=3600$. This selects $\beta=0$, with respective mean recalls of 92.82\%, 92.56\% and 92.30\% for the three values. The frozen test schedules therefore use predicted feasibility; work prediction acts as an auxiliary training target. Network parameters, sorting rules and source hashes are frozen before opening the test split.

\subsection{Matched-State Comparisons and Ablations}
Each test map supplies 18 matched inputs: three longitudinal positions $\{0,25,50\}$ m, three lateral positions $\{-5,0,5\}$ m, and two heading offsets $\{-0.15,0.15\}$ rad at nominal trim. This gives 576 inputs. Every method receives the same full state, target, map and preceding command.

Under a 12-candidate limit, we compare Cost, Diverse, Learned, Hybrid and No-geometry. Full-203 can search the complete feasible primary set in task-cost order, stopping at its first witness; it supplies the finite-library reference. A second comparison gives each of the five budgeted methods up to 203 candidates but imposes a hard limit of 3,600 continuation integration steps. Two repeats rotate method order, yielding $576\times11\times2=12{,}672$ timing observations. Repeat outcomes are checked for equality and merged before computing discovery rates.

Discovery recall is the fraction of Full-203-positive inputs on which a method obtains a witness. We also report consumed integration steps, planning-time quantiles and selected-primary cost relative to Full-203. Predicted work does not enter the test priority because the validation-selected exponent is zero. The geometry ablation isolates the value of the obstacle representation; Learned versus Hybrid isolates maneuver exploration. All learned timings include feature extraction and dense-network inference.

\subsection{Closed-Loop and Implementation Checks}
The 32 test maps are run under four execution conditions with Primary-3, Cost-12, Diverse-12, Learned-12, Hybrid-12 and Full-203, giving 768 episodes. Primary-3 applies the same primary checks but omits continuation validation. Episodes terminate at goal attainment, loss of every feasible primary, entry into inflated obstacle geometry, flight-envelope violation, invalid dynamics or timeout. All outcomes are reported. Minimum geometric clearance is measured against uninflated obstacles, distinguishing a planning-margin incursion from contact with an obstacle.

After the primary test suite, we conduct a frozen-model geometry-input ablation to examine why similar matched-state discovery counts accompany different candidate predictions. The already trained No-geometry model replaces the geometry model in Learned and Hybrid, adding 256 method--map--condition episodes on the same 32 maps. The four unaffected baselines are replayed to verify exact decision and trajectory reproduction. No model or scheduling parameter is changed. This post-evaluation ablation is reported separately from the primary 768-episode matrix.

Paired comparisons retain all conditions or states of a map together. Confidence intervals use 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 32 map blocks. Timing repeats and correlated states do not increase the number of independent maps.

The implementation passes 65 ROS tests covering geometry, continuation logic, exact work limits, the independent plant, neural inference and feature invariance, plus ten Python scheduling-replay tests. Exported network predictions are compared with PyTorch on 32 real validation examples per model; maximum absolute output error is $1.06\times10^{-5}$. Timed experiments run separately from Gazebo, training and compilation, pinned to one logical CPU of an Intel Core i5-12400F. The software uses Ubuntu 20.04, ROS Noetic and GCC 8.4 with \texttt{-O2}. Raw results, trained weights, split definitions and source hashes accompany the manuscript.
