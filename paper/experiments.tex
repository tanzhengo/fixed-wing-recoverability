\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Implementation and Common Constraints}
The implementation extends the local primitive planner at the pinned public revision in~\cite{hkpolyu2026code}. Each update generates 29 horizontal directions and seven altitude targets. The primary duration is $H=3$ s, and the continuation duration is $B=6$ s. The continuation library combines five horizontal direction fractions $\{0,-0.5,0.5,-1,1\}$ of the $45^\circ$ direction span with three altitude targets: level, upper reachable altitude, and lower reachable altitude. This produces 15 continuation controls. Changing a horizon also changes the command generator's target distance and gain; a 6 s primitive is therefore not simply a 3 s trajectory extended in time.

The nominal initial airspeed is 15.5 m/s at numerically solved level-flight trim. Roll is limited to $35^\circ$, climb and descent to $20^\circ$, and angle of attack to $[-20^\circ,25^\circ]$. Planner integration uses forward Euler with $\Delta t=0.02$ s. The control update interval is 0.2 s. The goal is $(250,0,20)$ m, with horizontal tolerance 15 m and altitude tolerance 3 m. The timeout is 40 s for every method.

All methods apply identical primary flight-envelope and collision checks. The comparisons are: \emph{Primary-3}, which selects the cheapest feasible 3 s primary; \emph{Primary-6}, which uses a 6 s primary; \emph{Cost-12}, which validates up to 12 endpoints in task-cost order; \emph{Diverse-12}, which applies the proposed schedule with the same candidate limit; and \emph{Full-203}, which searches the complete feasible primary set in cost order. Full-203 defines the finite-library continuation reference. The original implementation's less restrictive legacy mode is excluded from these ablations.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Evaluation Suite}
The map suite and parameters were fixed before evaluation. Four map families each contain six seeded instances: staggered pillars, a lateral obstacle wall, an offset cul-de-sac, and spatial obstacles combining finite-height cylinders with floating spheres. The first three families use altitude bounds of 16--24 m; the spatial family uses 5--45 m. A fixed integer pseudo-random generator determines positions and dimensions, and all resulting geometry is archived. The planner receives the complete static obstacle geometry. These maps test the effects of finite local prediction in a known environment.

Each map is run under four execution conditions. The nominal plant uses an independently implemented fourth-order Runge--Kutta integrator with 0.005 s steps. The model-mismatch condition multiplies actuator time constants by 1.25, lift coefficients by 0.97, and drag coefficients by 1.10. The wind condition adds a 1 m/s lateral ground-velocity component. The delay condition applies the preceding command for the first 0.1 s of each update and the newly selected command for the remaining 0.1 s. The planner retains its nominal model and receives the actual state at the next update. This gives $24\times4\times5=480$ distinct method--map--condition episodes. The complete-rollout and incremental validators are both run on this matrix to check decision and trajectory equivalence.

Episodes terminate at goal attainment, loss of every feasible primary, entry into inflated obstacle geometry, violation of the flight envelope, invalid dynamics, or the common timeout. Minimum geometric clearance is measured against the uninflated obstacles. This distinction keeps the planning collision margin separate from physical obstacle clearance. All outcomes enter the aggregate results.

\subsection{Matched-Input Budget Comparisons}
Closed-loop methods visit different states, so their mean episode timings combine algorithm cost with trajectory-dependent difficulty. To compare validation directly, we construct 432 matched inputs: 24 maps, three longitudinal positions $\{0,25,50\}$ m, three lateral positions $\{-5,0,5\}$ m, and two heading offsets $\{-0.15,0.15\}$ rad. Each input uses the same full state, target, obstacle map, and preceding control for every method.

In addition to Cost-12, Diverse-12, and Full-203, we compare cost and diversity schedules under hard continuation budgets of 3,600 and 10,800 integration steps. These correspond to approximately 12 and 36 complete 6 s continuation rollouts before early termination effects. They are upper limits on consumed work; a method that finds the cheapest feasible witness can finish earlier. Two timing repeats rotate execution order, yielding 6,048 observations per validator. Repeats are merged before computing discovery rates.

Continuation discovery recall is the fraction of Full-203-positive inputs for which a budgeted method finds a witness. We report paired disagreements, selected primary cost, numerical integration counts, and elapsed-time quantiles. Bootstrap intervals resample the 24 maps as blocks, keeping all states and execution conditions of a map together. This avoids treating repeated timing measurements or correlated states as independent map samples.

\subsection{Diagnostic and Reproducibility Checks}
The range-limited diagnostic executes a 17 m/s run with a 120 m spherical observation boundary and optional plan reuse. At every visited state, we query cache-free full-library and diversity variants with identical control history, both with and without the range restriction. Complete state snapshots and rejection reasons support this comparison. The diagnostic uses a 50 s limit and records whether arrival occurs before or after 30 s.

The implementation passes 55 tests: 19 inherited geometry and planning tests, 31 continuation and budget tests, and five independent-plant tests. The latter check level-flight trim, numerical convergence against analytic actuator dynamics, wind-induced ground-track change, and the response to parameter mismatch. Timed experiments run separately from Gazebo and compilation, with the benchmark pinned to one logical CPU on an Intel Core i5-12400F. The code uses GCC 8.4 with \texttt{-O2}, ROS Noetic, and Ubuntu 20.04. Source hashes, raw observations, and analysis scripts accompany the manuscript.
