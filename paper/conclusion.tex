\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented a learning-guided terminal continuation search for fixed-wing motion-primitive planning. A geometry-conditioned neural network learns continuation feasibility and validation work from explicit model-based checks. Its priority is combined with candidate and integration budgets, incremental constraint rejection, and an optional maneuver-diversity schedule.

On independent test maps, learned ranking improves witness recall from 94.39\% to 96.50\% under a 12-candidate limit and from 78.50\% to 87.15\% under a 3,600-step continuation budget, relative to diversity alone. Closed-loop completion increases from 70 to 99 tasks out of 128. A subsequent frozen-model input ablation completes 117 tasks without the obstacle-clearance channels, despite lower classification accuracy. This finding emphasizes the importance of evaluating learned representations through execution as well as prediction. Additional urban infill tests favor cost ordering over the learned schedules, showing that the observed completion gain depends on the environment family. The study also identifies the accompanying tradeoffs: inference increases planning time, fixed diversity interleaving does not improve every metric, and sampled continuation feasibility does not eliminate execution-margin incursions. Learning the allocation of explicit continuation checks provides a practical way to extend an existing fixed-wing primitive planner without changing its trajectory representation or control interface.
