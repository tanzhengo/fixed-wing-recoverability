\begin{abstract}
Fixed-wing local planners must retain room for subsequent maneuvers while allocating limited computation to trajectory validation. This paper presents a learning-guided scheduler for finite-library terminal continuation queries. A network trained from model-generated labels ranks primary endpoints by successor feasibility. The cheapest primary is checked first, and inference is deferred until that query fails; compact single-output inference removes inactive inputs and unused computation while preserving the selected control. A factorial study evaluates state and geometry inputs, auxiliary work supervision and three training seeds. The deployed configuration is selected by closed-loop performance on validation maps before testing on 32 new maps. A separate common RK4 check of the imminent control interval addresses numerical inconsistency between prediction and execution. Across 2,304 closed-loop episodes, the selected state-input network with this check completes 118 of 128 tasks, compared with 89 for maneuver diversity and 96 for full-library search under the same check, with no recorded inflated-boundary incursions for these three methods. Paired failure replay distinguishes numerical boundary crossings from terminal continuation scarcity. Fifteen known-map Gazebo trials further quantify the influence of altitude discretization and waypoint tolerance. The results support learning the allocation of explicit continuation checks and selecting its representation through execution-level validation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A fixed-wing aircraft must continue moving while its planner chooses the next maneuver. A trajectory that is collision-free over a short horizon can terminate with insufficient room for the subsequent turn or climb. Motion-primitive planners provide a structured way to handle vehicle dynamics, but selecting a primary trajectory solely by immediate task cost can overlook this loss of maneuvering space.

Two-phase fixed-wing planning combines global route guidance with local maneuver selection~\cite{tan2022twophase,tan2026twophase}. Within this hierarchy, a terminal continuation test evaluates a second control segment from the complete endpoint state of each primary. The additional look-ahead introduces a computational problem: checking $M$ successors for each of $N$ primaries requires up to $NM$ dynamic rollouts. A limited validation budget must therefore be allocated among candidates that differ in both their likelihood of admitting a continuation and the effort required to establish that result.

Task-cost ordering can spend its allowance on similar terminal motions. A learned schedule can prioritize endpoints likely to admit a continuation, but its utility depends on both the checks it saves and the cost of computing that priority. A further distinction arises in execution: a classifier trained to recognize nominal finite-horizon feasibility need not favor the same candidates as a policy trained for task completion. We therefore evaluate scheduling yield, total computation and closed-loop behavior as separate outcomes.

We develop a learning-guided continuation search that predicts finite-bank feasibility and allocates explicit checks. The implementation first validates the cheapest feasible primary and evaluates the network only when this common query fails. A compact inference model removes unused outputs and inactive input columns. Geometry encoding, auxiliary work supervision and maneuver interleaving are evaluated as design choices. The primary generator, nominal task objective and trajectory acceptance tests are shared with the baselines.

The contributions are threefold. First, we formulate learned allocation of finite continuation queries under candidate-count and integration-step limits. Second, we derive a deferred-inference implementation that preserves the selected control while avoiding network work on queries resolved by the cheapest primary. Third, we provide a factorial training ablation, independent-map evaluation, component-level runtime measurements and paired failure replay to identify where scheduling gains do and do not transfer to execution. A separate Gazebo study compares scheduling and altitude-library configurations in a known urban map. Its lidar stream records the observed environment; the planning input is the supplied collision geometry.

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Fixed-Wing Planning and Terminal Maneuvers}
Motion primitives and hierarchical route planning provide a computationally structured approach to fixed-wing navigation~\cite{tan2022twophase,tan2026twophase}. The implementation in this paper builds on the public three-dimensional local primitive planner in~\cite{hkpolyu2026code}, retaining its point-mass dynamics and actuator responses.

Terminal constraints in model predictive control connect finite-horizon optimization with closed-loop feasibility~\cite{mayne2000mpc}. Inevitable collision states characterize states from which all future collision-free motions have been lost~\cite{fraichard2004ics}. For fixed-wing navigation, Lim et al.~\cite{lim2024safe} connect trajectories to collision-free periodic circles, whose repeatability supports their safe-state construction. FASTER~\cite{tordesillas2022faster} maintains a backup trajectory within known free space while allowing a task trajectory to explore farther.

Our continuation query asks whether a primary endpoint admits a feasible successor from a specified control library over a specified duration. This finite witness fits the existing fixed-wing generator and provides a measurable validation task. The scheduling method allocates these queries; the periodic terminal maneuver and invariant-set constructions above address a different horizon of feasibility.

\subsection{Learned Primitives and Validation Effort}
YOPO~\cite{lu2024yopo} learns trajectory parameters and scores for a spatially anchored candidate set, while YOPOv2-Tracker~\cite{lu2026yopov2} extends this representation to integrated tracking and navigation. ORACLE~\cite{nguyen2022oracle} predicts collision costs for predefined velocity--steering action sequences from depth images and estimated motion, and combines these predictions with goal information to select a primitive. DPNet~\cite{zhang2026dpnet} predicts structural dead ends and prunes a compact trajectory library. These approaches establish the role of learned environment-dependent scores in selecting among multiple local maneuvers.

Learning can also target the computation performed by a planner. SaIL~\cite{bhardwaj2017sail} trains a node-expansion policy by imitating a fully informed search oracle, with search effort as its objective. Bayesian active edge evaluation~\cite{choudhury2018active} uses correlations among edge-validity outcomes to choose informative checks in a path library. Fastron~\cite{das2017fastron} instead learns a configuration-space collision proxy. These methods address different parts of the planning pipeline: search expansion, evaluation order, and the collision test itself.

We learn the priority of terminal continuation queries for a dynamically propagated fixed-wing primitive library. Each query asks whether the terminal state admits a feasible successor and how many integration steps the incremental validator is likely to require. The work label supplies an optional auxiliary training loss, whose effect is measured against feasibility-only training. Online ranking uses feasibility probability. Acceptance is determined by explicit continuation validation under the dynamics and geometric constraints. The comparison therefore concerns how much useful continuation evidence is obtained within a prescribed budget.
%% END SECTION: learning_related_work

\subsection{Maneuver Coverage}
Farthest-first selection spreads representatives across a metric space~\cite{gonzalez1985clustering}. We use this principle to diversify terminal headings and altitudes, normalized by nominal aircraft maneuver capability. Such physical scaling prevents small altitude variations in a narrow flight band from receiving the same importance as a substantial heading change. A hybrid ablation interleaves this coverage with learned priority. It tests the effect of exploration without assuming that fixed alternation improves task completion.

\subsection{Aircraft State and Local Planning}
The aircraft state is
\begin{equation}
 x=(p_x,p_y,h,V,\gamma,\psi,\phi,\alpha,T),
\end{equation}
where $h$ is altitude, $V$ is airspeed, $\gamma$ is flight-path angle, $\psi$ is heading, and $\phi$, $\alpha$, and $T$ are the roll, angle-of-attack, and specific-thrust actuator states. Each primitive holds the commanded input $u=(\phi_c,\alpha_c,T_c)$ constant for a duration $H$. First-order actuator dynamics maintain continuity across control switches. All internal angles and angular rates use radians and radians per second; degree values in the experimental setup are converted on input. The model is given in Appendix~\ref{app:model}.

At a planning update, the primary library $\mathcal U_H(x_0)$ contains $N$ regularly sampled controls. Numerical integration produces a sampled trajectory $\tau_i=\Phi_H(x_0,u_i)$ and terminal state $x_i^+$. Let $\mathcal X$ denote the flight envelope, $\mathcal F$ the collision-free region after obstacle inflation, and $\mathcal O(x_0)$ the region available for validation at the current update. The primary feasibility indicator is
\begin{equation}
 A_i=\mathbf 1\left\{\begin{array}{l}
 \forall x\in\tau_i:\quad x\in\mathcal X,\\
 p(x)\in\mathcal F\cap\mathcal O(x_0)
 \end{array}\right\}.
 \label{eq:primary}
\end{equation}
The experiments use the supplied obstacle map without a range restriction.

The primary task cost combines terminal distance, altitude error, terminal heading error, and command change:
\begin{align}
 J_i={}&w_d\|g-p_i^+\|+w_h|g_h-h_i^+|\nonumber\\
 &+w_\psi|\wrap(\psi_{g,i}-\psi_i^+)|
 +\|W_u(u_i-u_{\rm prev})\|_1 . \label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
Here $\psi_{g,i}$ points from the terminal position toward the target. The heading term is omitted within one meter of the horizontal target, and the command-change term is omitted before the first command. All methods use the same cost and primitive generator. If none of the regularly sampled primaries is feasible, they also test the preceding command over the full primary duration before declaring planning failure.

\subsection{Finite-Library Continuation Feasibility}
For each feasible primary, a terminal-dependent library $\mathcal B_B(x_i^+)$ contains $M$ continuation controls of duration $B$. The indicator
\begin{equation}
 C_i=\mathbf 1\left\{\begin{array}{l}\exists v\in\mathcal B_B(x_i^+),\ \forall x\in\Phi_B(x_i^+,v):\\
 x\in\mathcal X,\quad p(x)\in\mathcal F\cap\mathcal O(x_0)\end{array}\right\}
 \label{eq:continuation}
\end{equation}
uses the same sampled state and collision checks as~\eqref{eq:primary}. The continuation starts with the full terminal state, including actuator states; it does not restart at trim. Its observation boundary remains centered at the original planning state.

The nominal finite-library reference solves
\begin{equation}
 i^*=\arg\min_{i:A_i=1,\ C_i=1}J_i. \label{eq:full}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:full} ranks verified candidates by nominal task progress. It does not maximize geometric clearance or the number of feasible successors. The implementation checks continuation controls in a fixed order and stops checking a primary when it finds one feasible successor. The resulting pair is a finite continuation witness. When no witness is found within the available budget, the planner selects the cheapest feasible primary and records the unresolved continuation status. If the primary set is empty, the evaluation records a planning failure.

Two budgets are considered. A candidate budget $K$ limits the number of primary endpoints submitted for continuation validation. A work budget $W$ limits the total numerical integration steps spent on continuations. The latter accounts for rollouts that terminate early because of invalid dynamics. An unfinished rollout is never accepted as a complete witness. Primary propagation and schedule construction are included in elapsed planning time, but are reported separately from the continuation work budget.

\section{Budgeted Continuation Validation}
\subsection{Cost and Maneuver-Coverage Baselines}
Cost ordering sorts feasible primaries by $J_i$. Diverse starts from the same cheapest primary and then uses farthest-first coverage of $z_i=(\psi_i^+,h_i^+)$. With $V_0=\max(V_{\min},V(x_0))$, its scales are $s_\psi=\min(\pi,g\tan\phi_{\max}H/V_0)$ and $s_h=V_0H\max(\tan\gamma_{\max},\tan|\gamma_{\min}|)$. The distance and next query are
\begin{align}
 d(i,j)^2&=\left(\frac{\wrap(\psi_i^+-\psi_j^+)}{s_\psi}\right)^2
           +\left(\frac{h_i^+-h_j^+}{s_h}\right)^2,\label{eq:metric}\\
 s_{k+1}&=\arg\max_{i\in\mathcal I\setminus S_k}\min_{j\in S_k}d(i,j).
 \label{eq:schedule}
\end{align}
Zero scales deactivate the corresponding coordinate. Ties use task cost and original index. Physical scales prevent compressed altitude ranges from dominating coverage. These coordinates determine query order only; acceptance uses the full dynamic state. The minimum-cost verified member of the checked set is selected. Cost ordering can stop at its first witness; other schedules can stop when the globally cheapest primary passes, or after consuming their allowance.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Deferred learned continuation selection}
\label{alg:search}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require State $x_0$, target $g$, primary library, budget $K$ or $W$
\State Propagate primaries and retain feasible set $\mathcal I$
\If{$\mathcal I=\varnothing$ and a preceding control exists}
 \State Test that control over $H$; add it if feasible
\EndIf
\If{$\mathcal I=\varnothing$}\State \Return planning failure\EndIf
\State Let $s_1$ be the cheapest feasible primary
\State Validate successors of $s_1$; debit the query and work budget
\If{a complete witness is found}\State \Return $s_1$ and witness\EndIf
\If{budget is exhausted}\State \Return $s_1$ with unresolved status\EndIf
\State Extract features and rank the remaining primary endpoints
\State $i_{\rm best}\gets\varnothing$
\For{each remaining scheduled primary $i$ within budget}
 \State Validate its successors with incremental rejection
 \State Debit query and integration work; reject truncated rollouts
 \If{a complete witness is found and $J_i$ improves the best cost}
  \State Store $i$ and its witness as $i_{\rm best}$
 \EndIf
 \If{cost ordering found a witness or budget is exhausted}
  \State \textbf{break}
 \EndIf
\EndFor
\If{$i_{\rm best}\ne\varnothing$}\State \Return primary and witness
\Else\State \Return $s_1$ with unresolved continuation status
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Maintaining the closest selected distance for each endpoint gives $O(NK)$ farthest-first updates for a $K$-candidate prefix, in addition to $O(N\log N)$ task-cost sorting. Learned schedules also incur feature extraction and network inference. Primary propagation costs $O(NH/\Delta t)$. Continuation work scales as $O(KMB/\Delta t)$ in candidate-budget mode and is bounded by $W$ integration steps in work-budget mode. These operation counts expose an important distinction: the same number of checked primary endpoints need not entail the same amount of validation work.

\subsection{Incremental Constraint Rejection}
A continuation is feasible only if every sampled state satisfies its constraints. Once a sample violates a constraint, further integration cannot change the rejection. The incremental validator therefore terminates at the first violating sample and allocates the remaining work to subsequent controls or primary endpoints. A work-limit truncation is treated separately from a constraint rejection.

For a fixed candidate schedule without work truncation, this modification preserves the accepted continuation set and the selected primary. Both validators use the same deterministic integration prefix; a rejected prefix cannot become an accepted complete trajectory, while a feasible continuation is integrated to the same terminal sample. Under a hard work limit, the modification can change the decision by allowing the search to inspect more of the scheduled candidates. All learned and nonlearned schedules in the present comparison use this same incremental validator.

\subsection{Short-Prefix Prediction Consistency}
To test numerical sensitivity separately from scheduling, an optional common filter checks the next control interval with a higher-order nominal prediction. Let $\Delta_c=0.2$ s and let $\Phi^{\mathrm{RK4}}_{\Delta_c}$ use 0.005 s integration steps. The refined primary indicator is
\begin{equation}
 A_i^{\mathrm{p}}=A_i\,\mathbf 1\left\{\begin{array}{l}
 \forall x\in\Phi^{\mathrm{RK4}}_{\Delta_c}(x_0,u_i):\\
 x\in\mathcal X,\quad p(x)\in\mathcal F
 \end{array}\right\}.
\end{equation}
Only primaries passing the original Euler checks incur this extra test. Every schedule receives the same refined feasible set. The check addresses numerical agreement over the imminent execution interval; it does not estimate disturbances or replace the finite continuation horizon. Experiments report the filter both off and on, so its effect is not assigned to learned ranking.

\subsection{Search Status}
The implementation distinguishes finding a witness, exhausting a budget, exhausting the finite continuation search, and having no feasible primary. Rejected continuations are classified by invalid dynamics, inflated-obstacle intersection, flight-envelope violation, and observation-boundary violation. Complete-rollout validation assigns reasons in this priority order. Incremental rejection records the first violating sample, resolving simultaneous violations by envelope, collision, and observation boundary; model-invalid states are rejected first. Truncation by a work limit is recorded separately.

\section{Learning to Allocate Continuation Checks}
\subsection{Geometry and Dynamic-State Encoding}
For each feasible primary, an encoder represents the initial state, terminal state, target and flight envelope in 28 scalar channels. A geometry-augmented variant appends 75 obstacle-clearance channels, giving a 103-dimensional vector $\xi_i$. Both representations use the same validator-generated labels. During training, State masks the geometry inputs in the common 103-input architecture; deployment removes these inactive columns.

Twenty-eight scalar channels describe the initial and terminal airspeed, flight-path angle, roll, angle of attack, thrust and yaw rate; terminal displacement in the initial heading frame; the sine and cosine of relative terminal heading; target displacement in the terminal heading frame; upper and lower altitude margins; the two prediction durations; observation radius and normalized terminal displacement; primary cost; and vertical clearance. Yaw rate is an additional stored motion input, rather than an independent component of the nine-state dynamics. Fixed physical scales normalize these quantities as specified in Appendix~\ref{app:features}. Absolute world position and absolute heading are omitted, so the encoding is invariant to a common horizontal rigid transformation of the state, map and target.

The remaining 75 channels sample signed obstacle clearance on a grid attached to the terminal heading. Forward offsets are $\{0,22.5,45,67.5,90\}$ m, lateral offsets are $\{-60,-30,0,30,60\}$ m, and height offsets are $\{-15,0,15\}$ m. For probe $q$, the feature is
\begin{equation}
 c(q)=\clip\!\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal F}(q)}{30\,{\rm m}},-1,1\right),
\end{equation}
where signed clearance is positive outside the inflated obstacles. This representation captures free turning space and nearby barriers without integrating a continuation. The encoder and explicit validator use the same obstacle dimensions and margins. Altitude margins and vertical clearance are encoded separately.

\subsection{Feasibility Learning and Auxiliary-Loss Ablation}
A multilayer perceptron contains two ReLU hidden layers of widths 64 and 32, followed by two linear outputs. With $\rho$ denoting ReLU,
\begin{equation}
 (\ell_i,r_i)=W_3\rho\!\left(W_2\rho(W_1\xi_i+b_1)+b_2\right)+b_3.
\end{equation}
The common training architecture contains 8,802 parameters. The first output predicts continuation feasibility as $\hat p_i=\operatorname{sigmoid}(\ell_i)$. The second predicts log validation work, giving $\hat w_i=\exp(\clip(r_i,0,12))-1$ integration steps.

Training labels come from the same finite continuation bank used online. For every primary that passes the Euler feasibility checks, the incremental validator tests its 15 successors in the fixed library order. Training labels do not use the later online RK4-prefix filter. The binary label $C_i$ records whether a witness is found. The work label $w_i$ records all integration steps consumed up to the first witness or exhaustion of the bank. Thus, negative queries also provide work supervision. The training loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i\left[
 \operatorname{BCE}(\ell_i,C_i)
 +\lambda_w\operatorname{Huber}_1(r_i-\log(1+w_i))\right].
 \label{eq:learning_loss}
\end{equation}
Candidate examples are weighted uniformly. We compare $\lambda_w=0$ and $0.1$ using matched initializations and a common validation-BCE checkpoint criterion. The auxiliary output is removed for deployment because it is not used by the selected priority. This supervision requires obstacle geometry and model-based validation, but no expert flight trajectories.

\subsection{Feasibility Priority and Deferred Inference}
The online priority is $a_i=\hat p_i$. The original implementation also evaluated $\hat p_i/(1+\hat w_i/1000)^\beta$ with $\beta\in\{0,0.5,1\}$; validation favored $\beta=0$. The inference path therefore omits the work output. A state-only export additionally removes the 75 first-layer columns whose weights are zero after masking. The resulting networks have dimensions $28$--$64$--$32$--$1$ or $103$--$64$--$32$--$1$, with 3,969 or 8,769 parameters. This transformation changes neither the feasibility logit nor the training procedure.

All schedules start with the cheapest feasible primary $s_1$. We validate this candidate before constructing a learned schedule, charging its work to the same budget. If it has a witness, it is already the minimum-cost verified choice and inference is unnecessary. If it fails and budget remains, the network constructs the same ordering of the remaining candidates as the eager implementation. Learned ranking uses descending $a_i$, with task cost and original index resolving ties. Hybrid alternates this ordering with farthest-first coverage; it is retained as an ablation.

The deferred procedure preserves control selection for a fixed model and budget. When $s_1$ passes, both implementations terminate at $s_1$. Otherwise they validate the same remaining sequence with the same residual budget. This equivalence covers the selected control and consumed validation budget. We verify equivalence against the archived implementation and measure runtime with instrumentation disabled. Separate instrumented runs resolve primary propagation, scalar and geometric features, network inference, scheduling and continuation validation.

The minimum-cost verified primary is executed. Finite-bank labels indicate nominal successor existence, so improved classification need not increase execution clearance or task completion. We select the deployed training configuration using closed-loop validation maps and report all factorial alternatives on untouched test maps.

\section{Experimental Design}
\subsection{Common Planner and Execution Model}
The benchmark library contains 29 horizontal directions and seven altitude targets, giving up to 203 primary controls of duration $H=3$ s. Fifteen continuation controls combine five horizontal direction fractions $\{0,-0.5,0.5,-1,1\}$ of a $45^\circ$ span with level, upper and lower altitude targets over $B=6$ s. Initial airspeed is 15.5 m/s at numerically solved level-flight trim. Roll is limited to $35^\circ$, climb and descent to $20^\circ$, and angle of attack to $[-20^\circ,25^\circ]$. Primary and continuation prediction use forward Euler with a 0.02 s step.

The goal is $(250,0,20)$ m with 15 m horizontal and 3 m altitude tolerances. Controls are updated every 0.2 s; the timeout is 40 s. An independent execution plant integrates the nominal point-mass equations with RK4 at 0.005 s. Four conditions are tested: nominal dynamics; actuator time constants multiplied by 1.25, lift by 0.97 and drag by 1.10; a lateral ground velocity of 1 m/s; and a 0.1 s command delay. In the delay case the preceding command acts for 0.1 s and the new command for 0.1 s. The planner receives the actual state at each update. All methods use the same static map, candidate library, constraints and task cost. Cache reuse is disabled.

\subsection{Data Splits and Factorial Training}
Four procedural families contain staggered pillars, a lateral wall, an offset cul-de-sac, and finite-height cylinders with floating spheres. The first three have altitude bounds 16--24 m; the spatial family uses 5--45 m. Independent seed ranges define 160 training maps, 32 validation maps, 32 diagnostic maps and 32 held-out test maps. Each split is balanced across the four families. The diagnostic split supplies the frozen-model comparisons and failure analysis; the held-out split evaluates the implementation and model-selection protocol.

Training and validation sample 24 states per map: $p_x\in[0,65]$ m, $p_y\in[-12,12]$ m, $h=20$ m, heading within $\pm0.3$ rad, speed 14.8--16.2 m/s, flight-path angle within $\pm0.04$ rad and roll within $\pm0.18$ rad. Attack is perturbed by at most 0.015 rad from trim and thrust by 10\%. These states yield 298,418 training and 53,117 validation candidate examples. Every candidate label comes from the same explicit 15-control bank. The original sampler sets initial yaw rate to zero; the factorial study retains these data to isolate the input/loss ablation, and separately measures support differences during execution.

The factorial experiment crosses State/Geometry inputs with $\lambda_w\in\{0,0.1\}$ at seeds 20261030--20261032. Each run uses Adam for 60 epochs, learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-5}$ and batch size 2,048. Paired loss variants share their initialization and batch ordering. Every checkpoint is selected by validation BCE, avoiding a comparison of unlike aggregate losses. Candidate recall is measured by replaying validation labels under $K=12$ and $W=3600$; all seed-level results are retained.

\subsection{Execution-Based Model Selection}
Two validation criteria are compared. The query-only criterion maximizes the three-seed average recall across the two budgets, with mean BCE breaking ties. The execution criterion evaluates the four configurations at the fixed deployment seed 20261030 on the 32 validation maps under all four conditions, giving 512 episodes. It maximizes completion count, then minimizes margin incursions, then uses validation recall and BCE to resolve ties. The protocol and selected model are recorded before generating the held-out test maps. Both criteria and all four factorial models are reported, so the choice of criterion is visible in the results.

\subsection{Prediction-Consistency Ablation}
The original predictor and execution integrator differ near obstacle boundaries. We first replay the archived failures with identical initial state and control, comparing Euler at 0.02 and 0.005 s with nominal RK4 at 0.005 s over the actual 0.2 s command interval. Disturbance and delay are then applied separately. This diagnostic uses original outcomes, rather than new test outcomes, to define a short-prefix revalidation rule.

The rule re-integrates the next 0.2 s of every Euler-feasible primary with nominal RK4 and applies the same envelope and collision constraints. It uses no true wind or perturbed-model parameters. We test this common filter both off and on for every method in the held-out split. The original nominal task cost and continuation test are unchanged. This isolates a prediction-consistency correction from the scheduling contribution.

\subsection{Matched Queries, Closed Loop and Runtime}
Each new test map supplies 18 matched inputs: $p_x\in\{0,25,50\}$ m, $p_y\in\{-5,0,5\}$ m and heading $\pm0.15$ rad at nominal trim, giving 576 inputs. Cost and Diverse are compared with learned ordering under $K=12$ and $W=3600$. Full-203 searches in task-cost order until its first witness and supplies the finite-library discovery reference. Eager, deferred and compact versions of each relevant model are compared on identical queries. Model-dependent discovery and decision differences are kept separate from implementation equivalence.

The held-out closed-loop matrix contains Cost, Diverse, Full, the two original frozen learned models and the four factorial models: nine methods, 32 maps, four conditions and two prefix-filter settings, totaling 2,304 episodes. Outcomes are goal attainment, loss of every feasible primary, entry into inflated geometry, envelope violation, invalid dynamics and timeout. Every failure is counted. The four factorial methods use deferred single-output inference; the state variants also omit the inactive geometry computation.

End-to-end timing is measured with instrumentation disabled and method order rotated across repeated matched inputs. A separate instrumented pass separates primary integration, other primary work, scalar features, geometry probes, network inference, schedule construction, continuation validation and residual overhead. The optional RK4-prefix filter is timed separately. Runs are pinned to one logical CPU of an Intel Core i5-12400F and isolated from training, Gazebo and diagnostic replay. The software uses Ubuntu 20.04, ROS Noetic and GCC 8.4 with \texttt{-O2}. Candidate count and integration steps are operation budgets, not wall-clock deadlines.

\subsection{Paired Diagnostics and Implementation Checks}
The archived Geometry, No-geometry, Diverse and Full trajectories are replayed for 512 method--map--condition tasks. We require exact reproduction of outcomes, control-step counts and final goal distances. Failure predecessors are compared at identical states; a second probe locates the first Geometry/No-geometry command divergence along their shared trajectory. It records task cost, minimum predicted primary clearance and the feasible fraction of the 15 continuations. Distance probes saturate at 30 m, matching the feature encoder. These outcome-conditioned diagnostics explain the original suite and are not counted as independent test tasks.

Paired performance comparisons resample whole maps, retaining all conditions within a map, with 10,000 bootstrap draws. Three training seeds measure training variability and do not increase the number of independent test maps. Numerical export checks compare PyTorch and C++ logits; replay checks verify that deferred inference and removal of zero input columns preserve the selected command, witness status and validation work. Raw records, protocols and model/source hashes accompany the results.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Representation and Auxiliary-Loss Ablation}
Table~\ref{tab:factorial} and Fig.~\ref{fig:training} report the twelve training runs. Geometry reduces validation BCE from approximately 0.282 to 0.076 and improves continuation discovery on the sampled validation inputs. Auxiliary supervision has a smaller, budget-dependent effect: for Geometry, it increases mean $K=12$ recall from 97.55\% to 98.18\%, while reducing $W=3600$ recall from 87.84\% to 87.21\%. For State, the corresponding changes are 96.09\% to 95.95\% and 86.23\% to 85.46\%. Thus the auxiliary loss does not uniformly improve query allocation. The online implementation uses only the feasibility output.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments separate finite-library discovery, execution consistency and mission completion. Geometry is useful for predicting a nominal feasibility label, while the validation-selected State model yields better completion on the new procedural test maps. Lower classification error alone does not establish a better closed-loop schedule. Auxiliary work supervision and fixed maneuver interleaving likewise require direct measurement; neither provides a uniform improvement across the reported criteria.

Deferred inference addresses an implementation cost that is independent of learning quality. Every tested schedule begins with the same cheapest primary. Resolving that query first avoids feature and network work when its witness already determines the answer. Removing inactive columns and the unused output further reduces inference work without changing the feasibility logits. These changes preserve the original decision rule and can therefore be evaluated separately from a newly trained model.

The nominal objective still favors task progress among explicitly verified candidates. First-divergence replay shows that this can reduce the number of available continuations, but does not consistently reduce primary clearance. The observed boundary crossings instead motivate checking the executed prefix with a consistent numerical integrator. This correction uses the nominal RK4 equations shared with the execution plant; unmodeled wind, actuator mismatch and delay remain separate sources of prediction error. A finite witness covers only the sampled primary and successor horizon. Recursive feasibility would require an additional terminal construction, such as a repeatable loiter maneuver.

The current training distribution covers a narrow initial-state region, and closed-loop roll and yaw-rate observations extend beyond it. Broader state sampling and trajectory-level training are consequently relevant extensions. The city trials further show the influence of waypoint tolerance and altitude discretization. They evaluate known-map planning with a parallel lidar observation stream; planning from a partial sensor map and evaluation with six-degree-of-freedom flight dynamics remain separate system developments.

\section{Conclusion}
This paper presented learned allocation of finite terminal-continuation queries for fixed-wing primitive planning. Deferred inference and compact single-output export preserve the original decision rule while removing unnecessary feature and network work. A controlled input/loss study shows that geometric classification accuracy, query recall and closed-loop completion favor different configurations; execution validation selects the State+Aux model before independent testing.

On the new maps, this model completes 107/128 tasks without the numerical-prefix check and 118/128 with it. Under the same enabled check, Diverse and Full complete 89 and 96. Controlled replay identifies numerical prediction inconsistency in eighteen of twenty-one archived margin events, supporting a separate correction shared by every scheduler. The fifteen city trials expose the effect of altitude-library and waypoint-gate choices on deployment outcomes. These results locate the contribution in efficient allocation of explicit finite-horizon checks, with model selection, numerical execution and system configuration evaluated independently.

\section{Prediction Model and Primitive Construction}\label{app:model}
Let $N_a=T\sin\alpha+kV^2C_L(\alpha)$, with $k=1.86$, $g=9.81$ m/s$^2$, $C_L(\alpha)=0.03\sin\alpha+0.028$, and $C_D(\alpha)=0.001\alpha^2+0.002$. The point-mass prediction model is
\begin{align}
 \dot p_x&=V\cos\gamma\cos\psi,&\dot p_y&=V\cos\gamma\sin\psi,\\
 \dot h&=V\sin\gamma,\\
 \dot V&=T\cos\alpha-kV^2C_D-g\sin\gamma,\\
 \dot\gamma&=(N_a\cos\phi-g\cos\gamma)/V,\\
 \dot\psi&=N_a\sin\phi/(V\cos\gamma),\\
 \dot\phi&=2(\phi_c-\phi),&\dot\alpha&=\alpha_c-\alpha,\\
 \dot T&=10(T_c-T).
\end{align}
The evaluation plant independently implements these equations using double-precision RK4 integration. Under wind, the prescribed horizontal wind components are added to $\dot p_x$ and $\dot p_y$. Model perturbations change the aerodynamic multipliers and actuator time constants only in this plant.

For a primitive target $(d,\theta,h_w,V_w)$, the inherited generator uses $a_g=2V/d$, $\gamma_c=\operatorname{atan2}(h_w-h,d)$, and $\dot\gamma_d=a_g(\gamma_c-\gamma)$. Before saturation, the roll command is
\begin{equation}
 \phi_c=\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(2V^2\sin\theta\cos^2\gamma,
 d(g\cos\gamma+V\dot\gamma_d)\right).
\end{equation}
With $L=kV^2\cos\phi_c$, the attack command is obtained from
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_c=\arcsin\!\left(
 \frac{(V\dot\gamma_d+g\cos\gamma_c)/L-0.028}{0.03+T/L}\right),
\end{equation}
and the thrust command from
\begin{equation}
 T_c=\frac{kV^2C_D(\alpha_c)+g\sin\gamma_c}{\cos\alpha_c}
       +2.2a_g(V_w-V).
\end{equation}
Commands and inverse-trigonometric arguments are clipped to their respective limits. Numerical denominator guards operate outside the normal tested envelope. The inherited target-shift heuristic uses $\tau=0.8$ s. For a target at polar offset $(d,\theta)$, define $\nu=\dot\psi\tau/2$ and $l=V\tau$. It computes
\begin{align}
 d'&=\sqrt{\max(0.01,d^2+l^2-2dl\cos(\theta-\nu))},\\
 c_\tau&=\clip\!\left(\frac{l-d\cos(\theta-\nu)}{d'},-1,1\right),\nonumber\\
 a&=s(\theta-\nu)\arccos c_\tau,\\
 \theta'&=\clip(\wrap(\pi-a-\nu),-\theta_{\max},\theta_{\max}),
\end{align}
where $s(z)=-1$ for $z<0$ and $s(z)=1$ otherwise. The generator then recomputes the command from $(d',\theta')$. This geometric heuristic is common to all methods; it is not a wind estimator. Each output command is held constant within its primitive.

The primary cost weights in~\eqref{eq:cost} are $w_d=0.02$, $w_h=0.15$, and $w_\psi=3$. The command-change weights for $(\phi_c,\alpha_c,T_c)$ are $(1,0.1,0.05)$. Upright cylinders use a 20\% radial inflation and 1 m vertical clearance. Spheres use the larger of 20\% radius and 1 m as isotropic inflation. Collision tests are evaluated at every integration sample.
%% END SECTION: implementation
\section{Feature Normalization}\label{app:features}
Initial and terminal $(V,\gamma,\phi,\alpha,T)$ are divided by $(20,0.35,0.61,0.44,20)$ in their SI units; yaw rate remains in rad/s. Terminal displacement in the initial heading frame is divided by $(100,100,30)$ m, and goal displacement in the terminal frame by $(250,250,50)$ m. Relative heading uses its sine and cosine. Lower and upper altitude margins use 50 m; $H$ and $B$ use 10 s; observation radius uses 200 m; cost uses 20; and vertical clearance uses 10 m. Terminal displacement norm is divided by observation radius when the radius is positive, and set to zero otherwise. Geometry channels divide signed clearance by 30 m and clip to $[-1,1]$. No fitted normalization statistics are used.
