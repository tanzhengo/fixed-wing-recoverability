\begin{abstract}
Fixed-wing local planners must select short trajectories while preserving room for subsequent maneuvering. Evaluating a continuation for every candidate increases verification cost, whereas checking only the lowest-cost candidates concentrates the search on similar terminal motions. This paper presents a maneuver-diverse terminal continuation search for motion-primitive planning. A cost-seeded farthest-first schedule distributes validation across terminal headings and altitudes normalized by aircraft maneuver capability. An incremental validator terminates each continuation at its first constraint violation, preserving the remaining work for other candidates. On 432 matched input states, a 12-candidate diversity schedule discovers 94.63\% of the witnesses available to full-library search, compared with 71.34\% for cost ordering. With incremental rejection and the same 10,800-step work limit, the corresponding recalls are 94.63\% and 67.76\%. In 480 closed-loop simulations covering nominal dynamics, parameter mismatch, crosswind, and command delay, diversity completes 56 of 96 tasks, compared with 37 for cost ordering and 67 for full-library search. The results show that maneuver coverage and early constraint rejection jointly improve the use of finite continuation-validation budgets.
\end{abstract}
